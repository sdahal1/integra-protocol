# Integra seminar compared with the book

This compares the supplied 127-page Integra Protocol v1.1 PDF with the 30-video seminar and its Basic v5, Standard v5 and Advanced v3 handouts. Video 01 explicitly says the seminar takes precedence in a discrepancy. The book is preserved as an earlier version.

## Basic and Standard are added (New procedures)

Book: PDF p.40 presents one four-question procedure, now described by the seminar as Advanced.

Seminar: Videos 01 and 11 introduce three methods. Basic v5 has eight questions; Standard v5 adds the second identity, merging checks and a return branch.

Companion: Separate practice flows and handout readers, rather than adding their questions inside the old book loop.

Seminar sources: integra-01, integra-11, integra-ip-basic-method-v5, integra-ip-standard-method-v5

## The initial topic is more narrowly defined (Changed starting rule)

Book: PDF pp.40–41 accepts images, thoughts, emotions or bodily sensations as initial content; p.93 permits positive content while preferring a negative first reaction.

Seminar: Video 12 specifies a current negative emotion plus the situation: “I feel this about that.” Later Advanced content may still use any of the four elements.

Companion: The seminar setup explains this distinction and never invents a feeling to satisfy it.

Seminar sources: integra-12, integra-25

## Rehearse, then Basic, Standard, Advanced (New staged curriculum)

Book: PDF pp.31–35 gives introductory exercises before the single protocol.

Seminar: Videos 09–10, 18 and 23 specify the positive-experience rehearsal and at least three Basic and three Standard applications before moving on.

Companion: A dedicated rehearsal and a visible learning sequence; progression is guidance, not a mandatory unlock.

Seminar sources: integra-09, integra-10, integra-18, integra-23

## Repeated goals and beliefs with stable identities (New procedural detail)

Book: PDF pp.42–44 cycles from an identity’s goal into new content. Beliefs are discussed conceptually.

Seminar: Basic and Standard explicitly repeat needs/intentions/goals and beliefs/conclusions/decisions from the same identity. Standard adds B and checks both together.

Companion: Repeat buttons preserve the reference, and Standard question 16 returns to question 5.

Seminar sources: integra-15, integra-20, integra-ip-basic-method-v5, integra-ip-standard-method-v5

## Ordinary identity with no noticed goal (Revised exception)

Book: PDF p.95 asks for an intention, then a message, then what happens now.

Seminar: Videos 25 and 27 go directly to what comes up now for an ordinary identity. Broader identifications without a goal have a separate endpoint discussion.

Companion: The seminar Advanced flow uses its own exception. The older intention/message branch remains only in the book practice.

Seminar sources: integra-25, integra-27

## What gets written down depends on the method (Different recording rules)

Book: PDF p.103 says to write only the initial problem definition.

Seminar: Videos 15 and 20 record the topic and named identities for Basic/Standard; video 29 retains topic-only notes for Advanced.

Companion: Basic/Standard name fields and optional extra notes are distinguished; Advanced permits quiet practice.

Seminar sources: integra-15, integra-20, integra-29

## The second book closing question is absent (Simplified closing checklist)

Book: PDF pp.40 and 51–52 includes “anything else?” and “what would you like?” as two additional questions.

Seminar: Basic v5 lists a result check. Standard v5 and Advanced v3 list the result check plus “anything else?” They do not list the book’s second additional question.

Companion: Closing questions follow the selected handout; practical action planning is labeled an app reflection, not a missing source step.

Seminar sources: integra-ip-basic-method-v5, integra-ip-standard-method-v5, integra-ip-advanced-method-v3

## Existing concepts gain demonstrations (Further explanation, not a new principle)

Book: Identities, the four elements, internal conflict, present-time attention, exact wording, goal work and retesting already appear in the book (pp.11–30, 36–52, 98–109).

Seminar: Videos 04–09 explain those concepts again; nine live sessions demonstrate the three methods. The repeated-goal and felt-separation procedures are where the major operational additions lie.

Companion: The companion labels source stories and demonstrations, and avoids presenting the shared foundations as new discoveries.

Seminar sources: integra-04, integra-05, integra-06, integra-07, integra-08, integra-09, integra-14, integra-16, integra-17, integra-19, integra-21, integra-22, integra-24, integra-26, integra-28

## No pressure to force an endpoint (Companion adaptation)

Book: The book describes no-identity endpoints and a “withdraw behind” instruction on p.97.

Seminar: Video 27 urges insisting on an identity after repeated content answers; video 30 urges persistence in difficult processes. These are retained in the full source transcripts.

Companion: The guided interface instead allows clarification, pausing and ending without an endpoint. It does not automate the book’s withdrawal technique or press for an altered state.

Seminar sources: integra-27, integra-30
